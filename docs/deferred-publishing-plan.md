# Deferred publishing plan

**Status: deferred for future review.** Recorded November 23, 2026 against Plantigochi 0.1.2. Waz Lab’s preferred starting point is the public, account-free web app on GPT Sites. A web-only first year is one scenario. Store releases, dates, paid items and prices are proposals.

## Web launch and player saves

Players install from the game link and tend their plant on their device. The active plant, coins, inventory, journal and room layout live in IndexedDB. Players can share a garden postcard without sending anyone their save. For a new phone or computer, the existing export and restore flow can carry the garden in a file. JSON carries garden data and photo previews. A `.plantigochi` backup can also carry the available original framed photos. Make **Back up my garden** and **Restore my garden** easy to find before inviting a large audience. [Save system](../src/gardenStore.js) · [Backup format](../src/gardenBackup.js)

Keep the production web address stable because browser storage belongs to its origin. Progress can be lost if the device is lost or its app data is cleared. A backup kept elsewhere gives the player a recovery path. Player accounts, automatic cross-device garden sync and encrypted or device-locked save files were discussed and set aside. A deliberately portable file can be copied.

Before sharing with the plant group of roughly 500,000 members, test the two loads the web launch actually creates:

- **First installs.** Measure the initial download and cache-fill traffic during a burst of visits. Confirm the host’s bandwidth limits and costs against those measurements. Test a first install, an offline reopen, and an update that preserves a garden. Group membership alone does not predict simultaneous installs.
- **Optional reminders.** Most gameplay runs locally. The hosted Worker and D1 handle alert subscriptions and delivery. The documented scheduler runs hourly and the current sender selects up to 25 due reminders per dispatch. Test a burst of due alerts, measure the queue delay, and adjust dispatch capacity before relying on reminders for a large audience. Verify delivery on locked Android and iPhone devices. [Sender](../server/reminders.js) · [PWA and release guide](development/pwa-and-release.md)

**Save acceptance check.** Grow a seed, earn coins, place a pot, write a journal entry and frame a photo. Export the garden, restore it on another device and compare the plant’s growth and care timestamps, balance, placements, journal and original image. Request notification permission separately on the receiving device.

## Publishing routes

The React, Canvas and JavaScript game can be reused across the three stores. The first shared change for a packaged build would be launch detection. Today, gameplay opens only when the browser reports an installed PWA. A packaged copy needs to recognize its own launch and open setup or the saved plant directly. Keep browser visitors on the existing install tour. [App entry point](../src/App.jsx) · [Install detection](../src/installPrompt.js)

**Store administration to revisit.** Google Play lists a US$25 one-time registration fee. For personal accounts created after November 13, 2023, at least 12 testers must each stay opted into a closed test for 14 continuous days before the developer can apply for production access. Apple lists US$99 per membership year. TestFlight is optional, with no equivalent minimum tester count or duration for App Store submission. Steam lists a US$100 fee per product, recoupable after US$1,000 in adjusted gross revenue. For a developer’s first few titles, Valve currently lists a 21-day wait after paying the fee and at least two weeks with a public Coming Soon page. Recheck each store’s terms, fees, billing rules and the commercial distribution rights for bundled music before setting a launch date. [Google registration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435) · [Google testing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465) · [Apple membership](https://developer.apple.com/programs/whats-included/) · [TestFlight](https://developer.apple.com/testflight/) · [Steam onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding) · [Steam Direct fee](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee)

## Steam Deck, widescreen and controllers

Keep the leaf toy and iSEED phone in their original proportions. Let a 16:9 laptop show more of the room, and use the toy’s gentle zoom to make the LCD legible. At the Deck’s 1280 × 800 resolution the current desktop layout measured roughly 281 × 229 pixels for the LCD in a browser test. Judge readability at actual handheld distance. [Toy controls](../src/PocketDevice.jsx) · [Room controls](../src/Collector.jsx)

Give Steam Input a remappable default layout. Try D-pad to browse, A for Heart or confirm, B for Back, hold B to leave a minigame, and a shoulder button to switch between toy and garden. In the garden, move the highlight between objects, select a pot, browse valid placement circles and confirm or cancel. Keep prompts consistent with the active input. Android Bluetooth controllers could reuse the same game actions through the browser Gamepad API. Test a complete controller-only session from naming the seed through watering, placing the pot and playing Dew Catch. [Steam Input](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/steam_controller)

## Optional real-money items during the web year

Start with one permanent cosmetic in the iSEED shop if Waz Lab decides to sell anything. A **Ceramic Pot Pack for US$2.99** was an example for discussion. Keep the free Garden Coin rewards for visiting, watering and playing. A copied or restored local balance can replay a consumable top-up. Selling coins would need a separate design for tracking both purchases and spending. [Current shop](../src/GardenShop.jsx) · [Coin and item rules](../src/gardenEconomy.js)

1. Waz Lab would open its own Stripe merchant account, create a one-time product and price, and use Stripe **test mode** first. The iSEED shop would display the contents and cash price. Stripe-hosted Checkout can offer cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay on eligible devices. A player can check out as a guest. Stripe handles card entry and Waz Lab receives the payout. [Hosted checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/accept-a-payment?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted)
2. The Site Worker would create a pending order for a known product at its server-side price and return the checkout link. The installation would retain a private claim for that order before opening Stripe. Store payment credentials as server secrets. The player uses their own card or device wallet through Stripe. No ChatGPT Wallet connection is required.
3. On Stripe’s signed payment notification, verify the order, amount, currency and paid status. Record the purchase once in D1, even when Stripe retries. When iSEED reopens, check the pending order and unlock the pack in Inventory. A buyer who closes checkout early can still receive a successful purchase. [Stripe fulfillment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment)
4. Offer **Restore purchase** by sending a private, expiring link to the email recorded at checkout. Redeeming it proves access to that inbox. It would unlock the pack in the current garden. Ordinary garden backup and restore would continue to handle the plant, journal and photos. Keep purchase verification separate from editable inventory data. Define how refunds and lost receipts are handled before accepting money.

**Purchase acceptance check.** In test mode, complete checkout, close the app before the return page, reopen it and receive exactly one pack. Repeat a payment notification, cancel a second checkout, and restore the first purchase on another installation. Check that the buyer’s garden and Garden Coins survive each path. Review mobile and Steam digital-goods billing rules independently when porting the shop.

## When we pick this up

Start with the web backup round trip and reminder-capacity check. Then choose the next experiment based on demand: a single test-mode purchase, a signed Android test build, an iPhone TestFlight build, or a controller-ready Windows prototype. Each can be evaluated separately while the public web game continues to run.
